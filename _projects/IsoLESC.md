**Understanding the Water Budget in the Atmospheric Boundary Layer**

The atmospheric boundary layer (ABL) plays a crucial role in the global water cycle, exchanging water and energy with both the land surface and the free atmosphere. [cite: 14, 24] Understanding the processes governing this exchange is essential for predicting weather, climate, and the availability of water resources. [cite: 24] The Isotope-LSM-LES-Cloud (ISOLESC) modeling system provides a powerful tool for investigating these processes by combining high-resolution atmospheric modeling with the tracing capabilities of water isotopes. [cite: 40]

**Key Features of ISOLESC**

* **Large Eddy Simulation (LES):** Explicitly resolves turbulent eddies in the ABL, capturing the complex dynamics of mixing and transport.
* **Land Surface Model (LSM):** Simulates the exchange of water and energy between the land surface and the atmosphere, including soil evaporation, plant transpiration, and surface runoff. 
* **Cloud Microphysics:** Includes detailed parameterizations of cloud processes, such as condensation, evaporation, and precipitation formation. 
* **Isotope Fractionation:** Tracks the isotopic composition of water throughout the ABL, providing insights into the sources and pathways of water.

**Model Applications**

* **Quantifying the Water Budget:** IsoLESC can be used to determine the relative contributions of different sources and sinks of water in the ABL, such as rainfall, evaporation, and transpiration. [cite: 31]
* **Investigating Land-Atmosphere Interactions:** The model can be used to study how changes in land use or climate affect the exchange of water between the land surface and the atmosphere.
* **Improving Weather and Climate Prediction:** By providing a more detailed understanding of ABL processes, IsoLESC can help improve the accuracy of weather and climate models.
